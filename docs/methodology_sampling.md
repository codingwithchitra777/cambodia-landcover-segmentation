# Methodology note: spatial sampling of the study area

## Decision

The dataset is **not** a wall-to-wall map of Cambodia. It is a **systematic
spatial sample**: 58 square blocks of 20 × 20 km (400 km² each) placed on a
regular grid across the country, at 55 km centre-to-centre spacing. Together the
blocks cover **23,200 km², or 12.8% of Cambodia's 181,035 km²**. The ~87% of the
country between blocks is not exported or used.

Generated by `scripts/make_blocks.py` with `block_size_km=20`, `spacing_km=55`,
`min_land_frac=0.5`, clipped to the national boundary
(`USDOS/LSIB_SIMPLE/2017`). Each block has a stable id (`rRRcCC` = grid row/col).

## Why sample instead of covering the whole country

1. **Compute and storage budget.** Full wall-to-wall coverage at 10 m resolution
   would be ~22 GB of 6-band imagery and roughly 7,000 tiles. The project budget
   (see proposal §7.1) targets ~5 GB and free-tier cloud GPUs. The 12.8% sample
   is ~2.8 GB and ~885 tiles — trainable on Colab/Kaggle free tiers.
2. **Diminishing returns.** A model learns land-cover appearance from landscape
   *variety*, not from every pixel. A grid spread across all regions captures the
   same range of conditions (paddy plains, the Tonlé Sap basin, forested uplands,
   the coast, urban cores) as full coverage would, at a fraction of the cost.
3. **Systematic, not opportunistic.** A regular grid avoids cherry-picking
   convenient scenes. It gives even geographic representation and is
   reproducible from three parameters.

## Why this does not leak or bias the evaluation

- The train/val/test split is done **by whole block** (`scripts/split_blocks.py`),
  never by individual tile. Because blocks are 55 km apart, tiles in different
  blocks are effectively spatially independent — no train tile sits next to a
  test tile. This is the hard anti-leakage requirement in `CLAUDE.md`.
- The ~150 hand-corrected ground-truth tiles used for the headline evaluation are
  a separate, held-out asset layered onto the test blocks, not drawn from the
  weak-label training pool.

## Known limitation and mitigation

A sparse grid can under-sample **rare classes**. In particular:

- **Water** (Tonlé Sap lake, rivers, coast) and **built-up** (city cores) occupy
  a small fraction of the country and may fall largely in the grid gaps. For
  reference, of the tested landmarks only Phnom Penh (`r02c05`) and Kampong Cham
  (`r03c06`) land inside a block; Siem Reap, Battambang, the Tonlé Sap centre,
  Sihanoukville and Mondulkiri fall in gaps.

**Mitigation (planned):** after tiling, compute the actual per-class pixel
distribution across all tiles. If water or built-up is under-represented, add a
small number of **hand-placed blocks** over the Tonlé Sap lake, the coastline,
and Phnom Penh's core, and re-export just those. Topping up specific classes is
cheap (a handful of extra blocks) and keeps the class-balance decision grounded
in real counts rather than assumption.

## How to change the sampling density

The trade-off is controlled by `spacing_km` in `make_blocks.py`:

| `spacing_km` | Coverage | Approx. size |
|--------------|----------|--------------|
| 55 (current) | ~13% (gaps between blocks) | ~2.8 GB / ~885 tiles |
| 30 | ~40% (denser) | ~9 GB |
| 20 (= block size) | 100% (wall-to-wall) | ~22 GB |
